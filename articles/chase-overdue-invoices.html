The 4-stage system for chasing overdue invoices

Freelancers don't lose money on the work — they lose it on the awkwardness after the work. Here's the escalation system, with the exact emails. Copy them freely.

Pick the stage, not the mood

The mistake is writing each chaser from scratch, calibrated to how frustrated you feel that day. Instead: the calendar picks the email.

StageWhenPosture
1 · Nudgedue date → +5 daysAssume oversight, zero accusation
2 · Direct+6 → +14Clear ask with a specific date
3 · Firm+15 → +29Consequence + an offramp
4 · Final+30 and beyondCalm, factual, dated last notice

Stage 1 — the nudge

Subject: Invoice [NUM] — friendly nudge Hi [name], quick nudge: invoice [NUM] for $[X] was due [date] — it may have slipped past (happens to all of us). Payment details below for easy reference. If it's already on its way, ignore me!

Stage 2 — direct

Subject: Invoice [NUM] is [N] days overdue Hi [name], following up on invoice [NUM] ($[X], due [date], now [N] days overdue). Could you confirm payment will be made by [specific date, ≤5 days out]? If something's holding it up on your end, tell me and we'll sort it. I'd like to keep [project] on schedule, which needs this cleared.

The load-bearing part is the specific date. "Soon" is not a commitment; "by Tuesday" is.

Stage 3 — firm, with an offramp

Subject: Invoice [NUM] — action needed by [date] Hi [name], invoice [NUM] ($[X]) is now [N] days overdue and I haven't had a response to my last note. Per our agreement, [work pauses today / a late fee of $[Y] applies from [date]] until it's settled. I don't enjoy sending these — if cash flow is tight, I'm open to [2–3 installments, first by [date]]. Otherwise please confirm payment by [date].

The installment offer is the secret weapon: it converts "someday" into "something today," and clients in genuine trouble take it gratefully.

Stage 4 — final notice

Subject: Final notice — invoice [NUM] Hi [name], this is my final direct request regarding invoice [NUM] ($[X]), now [N] days overdue. If payment isn't received by [date, 7 days out], I'll have to [hand this to a collections service / pursue it through small claims / involve your accounts-payable team formally], which neither of us wants. I'd much rather resolve this between us this week.

Only threaten what you'll actually do. The calm tone is the threat.

Three rules that make it work

① Every email names the invoice number, amount, due date, and how to pay — no friction. ② Exactly one ask per email, with a date. ③ Never apologize for asking. You did the work.
